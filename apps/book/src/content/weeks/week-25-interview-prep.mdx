<Hook>Can you explain, implement, debug and defend your own work under a time limit without pretending to know what you do not?</Hook>
<Intuition>
An interview samples behavior under constraints. Practice four surfaces separately: coding, system design, fundamentals aloud, and behavioral/project discussion. A strong notebook does not automatically create a clear 90-second explanation, and memorized explanations do not repair a broken gradient.

Use your capstone plus two strongest assignments as evidence. Rehearse with a timer, preserve the recording or notes privately, and review against a rubric. Company-loop anecdotes are noisy and time-dependent; recruiter instructions outrank a blog post or this curriculum.

## The math, derived

### Allocate practice to evidence-backed weaknesses

For surface s, let a_s be attempted rubric items and c_s be correct items. A simple error estimate is 1-c_s/a_s; unattempted surfaces remain unknown, not mastered. Weight important surfaces and repeat mistakes after a delay rather than practicing only what feels fluent.

<MathBlock latex="e_s=1-\frac{c_s}{a_s},\qquad p_s=\frac{w_se_s}{\sum_jw_je_j}" />

This is a planning heuristic, not a validated model of hiring probability. Example: coding error .2 and fundamentals error .6 at equal weights allocate one-quarter versus three-quarters of targeted remediation time. Still schedule at least one rehearsal of every surface to avoid blind spots.

### Budget the mock loop

Time is part of the constraint. Four 35-minute rounds, three 5-minute breaks and a 45-minute review require 200 minutes. Reserve review before adding another mock; repeating an unexamined error reinforces it.

<MathBlock latex="T=4\times35+3\times5+45=200\ \mathrm{minutes}" />

## The code, from scratch

Start with the [coding companion set](/interview/coding-set) and the [interview dashboard](/interview). Then use `interview/PRACTICE.md`: three AI-assisted coding exercises, two timed take-homes and three debugging drills, each with acceptance evidence and a scoring rubric.

The debugging cases deliberately break disposable copies, not the canonical references: unshifted causal targets, future-derived features, and unequal-rank mean reduction. Diagnose each with a minimal failing regression before changing code. Never conceal assistant contributions when the assessment requires disclosure.

## The seven-day protocol

### D1 — Coding refresh

Select weak categories from a larger ML-flavored problem bank; do not attempt fifty full problems in one day. Reimplement a heap/top-k utility, vectorized softmax, a training step and one model component without copying. Explain time/memory complexity and edge cases. Complete one 45-minute AI-assisted task under a written assistance policy, then review every accepted suggestion.

### D2 — Fundamentals aloud

Record three 20-minute explanations: bias/variance and leakage; backprop and optimization; attention, tokenization and preference learning. Derive one equation on paper and define every symbol. Use a rubric for correctness, assumptions, examples and communication. Repeat the weakest explanation after a break without memorizing a script.

### D3 — Two system designs

Design a recommender and a RAG system at a stated workload. Begin with requirements, data/metrics and a baseline, then budget retrieval, ranking/generation, storage, cache, latency, evaluation and failure recovery. Avoid arbitrary “100k QPS” claims: derive capacity from assumptions and distinguish measured numbers from estimates.

### D4 — Three target companies

Read one current public paper/report and one job description for each of three targets. Use the [company matrix](/companies) to revisit technical axes and [/companies/compare](/companies/compare) to synthesize differences. Prepare evidence-backed questions and list unknowns. Verify the current loop with the recruiter; do not treat the seeded practice angles as leaked interview questions.

### D5 — Behavioral, values and recruiter call

Prepare eight real STAR-plus-reflection stories covering ambiguity, disagreement, failure, debugging, ownership, collaboration, prioritization and ethical judgment. Map stories to competencies without fabricating impact or borrowing someone else's experience. Rehearse a 60-second introduction and ask about role scope, assistance policy, location and timeline.

For Anthropic-related values discussion, read the current public Responsible Scaling Policy and separate your own reasoning from slogans. For Google DeepMind-related ethics discussion, analyze a concrete trade-off and uncertainty. Do not claim knowledge of a private scoring rubric. Templates and privacy guidance live in `interview/OUTREACH.md`.

### D6 — Full mock loop

Run four rounds covering the four surfaces, with one explicitly permitted AI-assisted coding segment. Have a peer—or a strict written rubric—score artifacts rather than confidence. Record one blocking misconception and one communication habit per round. Rehearse the 90-minute and four-hour take-homes from `interview/PRACTICE.md` in separate reserved blocks if the week cannot honestly fit both.

### D7 — Portfolio, outreach and logistics

Spend half the day polishing resume/LinkedIn and the three artifacts: capstone plus two assignments. Each project needs a working reproduction command, tests, evidence, limitations and a short demo. Draft a personalized, low-pressure outreach note; **do not send bulk messages automatically**.

Compare compensation across base, bonus, vesting, equity type and liquidity. Public aggregators such as levels.fyi are noisy samples, not offers; RSUs and profit-participation units are not interchangeable cash. Seek qualified advice for tax/legal specifics. Confirm timezones and daylight-saving offsets for Mistral/DeepMind London or other European interviews, along with location/visa constraints from the actual role.

<Gotcha>Completing this book does not guarantee interview readiness or employment. Use mocks and external feedback to find gaps; report uncertainty honestly rather than performing confidence.</Gotcha>

<Assignments>
- **Warmup (30 min):** score one recorded fundamentals explanation against a written rubric.
- **Build (2–3 hrs):** complete the 90-minute take-home and review its weakest criterion.
- **Challenge (3+ hrs):** run the full mock loop or four-hour take-home, preserve artifacts and plan targeted remediation.
</Assignments>
<InterviewDrill role="llm-product" time="30 min">
**“Explain your strongest project in two minutes, then implement and test its smallest critical component.”**

Baseline: truthful overview and working core. Senior: edge-case tests, complexity and trade-offs. Staff: evidence limits, failure diagnosis, privacy and communication under uncertainty. Worked rubrics: `interview/PRACTICE.md` and `interview/OUTREACH.md`.
</InterviewDrill>

<KeyTakeaways>
- Practice all four surfaces and remediate observed errors.
- Review every timed exercise instead of collecting attempts.
- Follow the real AI-assistance and privacy rules.
- Ground stories and portfolio claims in your own evidence.
- Treat outreach, compensation and scheduling as careful human decisions.
</KeyTakeaways>
