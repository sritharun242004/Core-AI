<Hook>Can three tiny models establish a scaling law, and what exactly can a toy preference experiment prove?</Hook>
<Intuition>
A scaling law is an empirical model of a family of experiments. A curve that fits three points is not automatically a law, particularly when several coefficients and an irreducible floor are unknown. A preference objective is easier to verify algebraically, but optimizing it on a tiny fixture does not make a language model safe or useful.

This second research week completes a three-paper study: Transformer mechanisms in Research α, **Chinchilla** allocation reasoning from Hoffmann et al. 2022 at DeepMind, and **DPO** from Rafailov et al. 2023 at Stanford. Keep the source, replication assumptions and local observations in separate columns. Read the Epoch AI Chinchilla replication discussion before treating fitted coefficients as universal constants.

## The math, derived

### 1. A bounded one-dimensional fit

Fix irreducible loss E explicitly and posit excess loss decreasing as a power of parameter count N. Taking logarithms yields a linear regression on log N:

<MathBlock latex="L(N)=E+AN^{-\alpha},\qquad \log(L-E)=\log A-\alpha\log N" />

For a generated fixture with E=1.5, A=4 and alpha=.3, four positive sizes recover the declared parameters up to numerical precision. That proves the fitter, not a property of language models. Changing E changes the fitted slope. Report that sensitivity rather than selecting the floor that produces the most attractive exponent.

### 2. Joint allocation needs a grid

A common stylized joint form adds a data term for D training tokens. Under an approximate dense-Transformer compute budget proportional to ND, optimizing allocation relates model and data budgets—but the fit needs experiments that vary both, with architecture and optimizer conditions controlled.

<MathBlock latex="L(N,D)=E+\frac{A}{N^\alpha}+\frac{B}{D^\beta},\qquad C\approx kND" />

The constant k depends on counting assumptions; measured wall time is not identical to theoretical FLOPs. Equal training tokens across models are unequal compute. Incomplete optimization, tokenizer changes, validation leakage and data quality can all masquerade as scaling effects.

The shipped `run_three_sizes` really trains three small **fixed-context MLP language models**, logs parameters/tokens and evaluates disjoint synthetic context IDs. It is not three Transformers on TinyStories, and its fitted slope is not a Chinchilla estimate. Larger models may overfit and produce a negative fitted exponent; retain that outcome.

### 3. Paired seeds and intervals

When comparing a baseline and an ablation under matched seeds, resample seed-level differences. The sampling unit is a complete run, not the thousands of correlated minibatches within it.

<MathBlock latex="d_s=m_s^{\mathrm{ablation}}-m_s^{\mathrm{baseline}},\qquad \bar d=\frac{1}{S}\sum_s d_s" />

Two or three seeds yield weak uncertainty estimates. Report their raw values, not just a narrow-looking plotted band. Select the metric and stopping policy before inspecting favorable results.

### 4. DPO as a reproducible objective

For prompt x and preferred/rejected completions y+ and y−, form the policy-minus-reference log-ratio margin. Reference probabilities are fixed. Beta scales the preference margin.

<MathBlock latex="z=\beta\left[\log\frac{\pi_\theta(y^+\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y^+\mid x)}-\log\frac{\pi_\theta(y^-\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y^-\mid x)}\right],\qquad L=-\log\sigma(z)" />

At z=0 the loss is log 2. Its chosen-score derivative is negative and rejected-score derivative positive, so gradient descent increases the relative chosen likelihood. This is **Stanford DPO**, not a method invented at another lab because authors later changed affiliations.

The toy policy completes each prompt with one token. Sequence DPO uses **summed completion log-probabilities**, causal shifting and masks; refer to Week 15a for those contracts. Do not substitute token means silently or accidentally update the reference. Preference improvement is not proof of truthfulness, fairness or safety.

## The code, from scratch

```python
import numpy as np
from scaling_dpo_repro import fit_power_law, run_three_sizes, train_preference

sizes = np.array([100, 200, 400, 800])
fit = fit_power_law(sizes, 1.5 + 4 * sizes ** -0.3, irreducible=1.5)
assert abs(fit['alpha'] - 0.3) < 1e-8
rows = run_three_sizes(seed=23)
result = train_preference(seed=23)
assert result['reference_unchanged']
```

The implementation isolates RNG state, uses explicit parameter counts, rejects unidentifiable or invalid one-dimensional inputs and permits unfavorable empirical slopes. No model, corpus or provider is downloaded.

### Optional mini-Chinchilla experiment

Use the Week 14 decoder with a fixed tokenizer and a licensed, versioned **local** TinyStories corpus. Choose three widths, at least two independent token budgets per size and several seeds; keep held-out stories untouched. Register architecture, context, optimizer, actual token exposures, theoretical FLOP assumptions, elapsed time and validation loss. Fit only the coefficients the design can identify. Compare a fixed-compute slice as well as fixed-data slices.

This extension is not executed by the reference notebook. The red compute tier refers to this larger experiment. Read the cost ceiling and teardown procedure before optional provisioning; publish incomplete or failed runs rather than fabricating a finished sweep.

<KeyTakeaways>
- A scaling fit is conditional on its experimental design.
- Three sizes do not identify every parameter/data coefficient.
- Preserve negative slopes and failed runs.
- DPO has a fixed reference and precise completion-scoring conventions.
- Research credibility comes from reproducible evidence and bounded claims.
</KeyTakeaways>
