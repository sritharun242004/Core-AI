<Intuition>
Multimodality is not one magic layer. An image encoder turns patches into vectors; a language encoder turns text into vectors; a voice system moves between audio, text, retrieval, and audio; and a generator learns a distribution over possible outputs. Retrieval adds another discipline: before answering, find evidence, rank it, measure it, and preserve its boundary in the answer. The interfaces between these steps matter as much as the individual models.

## The anatomy of a multimodal retrieval system

A useful system diagram is a contract diagram:

```text
image ──> visual encoder ──┐
                           ├─> shared or joined representation ──> retrieve/rerank
text  ──> text encoder ────┘                                      │
                                                                  v
spoken audio ─> ASR ─> query ─> evidence ─> answer ─> TTS ─> audio
```

Each arrow hides a failure mode. A patch tokenizer can lose small objects. A shared
embedding can retrieve a semantically close but wrong image. ASR can mis-transcribe a
proper noun. A retriever can miss the right paragraph. A reranker can overfit a lexical
cue. A generator can ignore a citation. TTS can render a confident but unsupported
answer. The offline project exposes each seam so an assignment can test one contract at
a time.

## 1. CLIP: make image and text comparable

An image is a grid of channels, height, and width. A vision encoder can produce one
vector from the whole image, or a sequence of patch vectors. A text encoder produces a
sequence and then pools it into a text vector. Contrastive training encourages the
matching image/text pair to be close and non-matching pairs in the batch to be apart.
For normalized image vectors `v_i` and text vectors `t_j`, a symmetric contrastive loss
can be written:

<MathBlock latex="s_{ij}=\frac{v_i^\top t_j}{\tau},\qquad \mathcal{L}_{I\rightarrow T}=-\frac{1}{B}\sum_i\log\frac{\exp(s_{ii})}{\sum_j\exp(s_{ij})}" />

The text-to-image direction swaps the axes, and a common objective averages both
directions. `\tau` is a temperature, often learned or configured. Normalization means
the dot product is a cosine similarity; without it, vector norm can dominate the score.
The batch supplies negative examples, so batch composition is part of the training
signal.

The Week 17 corpus does **not** train CLIP and does not contain images. Its `dense`
method is a deliberately listed alias feature map: `utterances` can map to `speech`,
and `images` to `image`, so a learner can inspect a semantic-ish retrieval contract
without a checkpoint. A real CLIP-like extension must name its model, tokenizer,
preprocessing, license, checkpoint, and evaluation set.

<Gotcha>“The image and text vectors are close” is not the same as “the caption is true.” Contrastive alignment is a retrieval signal. It can be sensitive to captions, prompts, resolution, demographic coverage, and the negative set.</Gotcha>

## 2. ViT: turn image patches into tokens

A Vision Transformer (ViT) treats an image as a sequence. For an image with height `H`,
width `W`, channels `C`, and square patch size `P`, the number of patches is:

<MathBlock latex="N=\frac{H W}{P^2},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{H\times W\times C}\rightarrow X\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times(P^2C)}\rightarrow E\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times d}" />

The patch projection is a learned linear map. Positional information says that the patch
at the top-left is not interchangeable with the patch at the bottom-right. A class token
can attend to patch tokens and provide a global representation. A self-attention block
then has the familiar shape:

<MathBlock latex="\operatorname{Attention}(X)=\operatorname{softmax}\left(\frac{QK^\top}{\sqrt{d_h}}\right)V" />

This is a representation choice, not a guarantee that all spatial details survive. A
small patch size raises the token count; a large patch loses local detail. Resolution,
augmentation, positional scheme, and pretraining objective alter the behavior. The
project's text corpus uses document ids rather than pixels, so its CLIP/ViT discussion
is an architectural map and its executable multimodal piece is a tiny array demo below.

## 3. VAE and GAN: two ways to model a data distribution

### Variational autoencoder

An autoencoder has an encoder and decoder. A variational autoencoder makes the encoder
predict a distribution, often diagonal Gaussian parameters `\mu(x)` and
`\log\sigma^2(x)`, then samples a latent with the reparameterization trick:

<MathBlock latex="q_\phi(z\mid x)=\mathcal{N}(\mu_\phi(x),\operatorname{diag}(\sigma_\phi^2(x))),\qquad z=\mu_\phi(x)+\sigma_\phi(x)\odot\epsilon,\quad\epsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)" />

The evidence lower bound (ELBO) is commonly optimized as a reconstruction term plus a
KL term in the loss convention:

<MathBlock latex="\mathcal{L}_{VAE}=\mathbb{E}_{q_\phi(z\mid x)}[-\log p_\theta(x\mid z)]+D_{KL}(q_\phi(z\mid x)\parallel p(z))" />

The KL term keeps the latent close to a prior, which makes prior sampling meaningful,
while the reconstruction term preserves information needed to decode. A VAE may trade
sharpness for a smooth and structured latent space. The project reports a sampled
latent, reconstruction MSE, and a diagonal-Gaussian KL on a tiny grayscale array. It
does not train an image VAE.

### GAN

A generative adversarial network has a generator `G` and discriminator `D`. In the
classic minimax framing, the discriminator separates real samples from generated ones
while the generator tries to make generated samples look real:

<MathBlock latex="\min_G\max_D\;\mathbb{E}_{x\sim p_{data}}[\log D(x)]+\mathbb{E}_{z\sim p(z)}[\log(1-D(G(z)))]" />

The game is not a normal reconstruction objective. Training can be unstable, the
discriminator can overpower the generator, and mode collapse can reduce diversity. A
successful GAN sample is also not proof that the training distribution was complete or
unbiased. Do not conflate GAN adversarial training with VAE variational inference or
diffusion denoising.

### Diffusion

A forward diffusion process adds noise over steps. In a simplified Gaussian schedule:

<MathBlock latex="q(x_t\mid x_{t-1})=\mathcal{N}(\sqrt{1-\beta_t}\,x_{t-1},\beta_t I)" />

A useful closed form samples a noisy state from the original image:

<MathBlock latex="x_t=\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}x_0+\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\epsilon,\qquad \epsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)" />

A learned denoiser predicts noise, a score, or a related parameterization and runs the
reverse process. The reference project takes one seeded noisy array and applies a local
neighbor mean. That is intentionally **diffusion-shaped**, not a trained U-Net, score
model, or image generator. It gives us a finite step and a tensor to test, not a quality
claim.

<Gotcha>A single finite denoising operation is not “a diffusion model.” The schedule, parameterization, conditioning, sampler, number of steps, training data, and evaluation all matter.</Gotcha>

## 4. World models and video models

A static image representation does not tell us how a scene changes. A video model must
represent spatial content and time. One abstraction introduces a latent state `s_t`, an
action or condition `a_t`, and a transition model:

<MathBlock latex="s_{t+1}\sim p_\theta(s_{t+1}\mid s_t,a_t),\qquad x_t\sim p_\theta(x_t\mid s_t)" />

A world model is often discussed as a model that predicts or simulates future states
for planning. A video generator may predict the next latent, denoise a whole temporal
window, or use factorized spatial/temporal attention. These labels overlap in practice,
but the evaluation question is different: does the model preserve identity, geometry,
motion, causality, and long-range consistency rather than only produce one plausible
frame?

The corpus includes a `video` document with explicit links to diffusion and voice. That
is a knowledge-graph teaching edge, not a video model. It cannot establish temporal
prediction, physical understanding, or a world model's planning utility. To extend the
project, add a tiny sequence fixture and test frame shape, time ordering, and a held-out
transition before measuring perceptual quality.

## 5. Audio and voice-native pipelines

A voice-native assistant is more than “speech in, text out.” A minimal pipeline is:

1. **VAD/ingress:** identify speech segments and accept an audio stream;
2. **ASR:** transcribe speech to text, with timestamps and uncertainty if available;
3. **query normalization:** preserve names, units, negation, and language information;
4. **retrieval:** search and rerank evidence with a bounded candidate budget;
5. **response policy:** answer, ask a clarification, or explicitly say evidence is missing;
6. **TTS:** synthesize the response with interruption and streaming behavior;
7. **telemetry:** record latency, errors, consent, retention, and citation state.

For a voice query `a`, the executable seam is:

```python
transcript = transcriber.transcribe(a)
hits = retriever.search(transcript, method="hybrid")
answer = answer_question(retriever, transcript)
audio = synthesizer.synthesize(answer.text)
```

The adapters in the reference project are honest stubs. `OfflineTranscriber` decodes
UTF-8 fixture bytes; it does not recognize speech. `OfflineSynthesizer` returns bytes
prefixed with `OFFLINE_AUDIO:`; it does not produce a waveform. Protocols make the seams
injectable, and tests can verify no API call is required. A real implementation must
address microphone permissions, speaker interruption, streaming backpressure, PII,
accent and language coverage, timeout behavior, and the difference between transcript
confidence and answer confidence.

## 6. Retrieval: lexical, dense, hybrid, and RAG variants

Let a corpus contain documents `d_1,\ldots,d_n` and a query `q`. A retrieval system
returns a ranked list `\pi(q)`. The answer generator should see only a bounded context
selected by a retrieval policy, and the final response should preserve document ids or
citations. “RAG” describes this retrieve-then-generate pattern; it does not guarantee
grounding.

### TF-IDF and cosine

Term frequency captures occurrences in one document; inverse document frequency reduces
the influence of ubiquitous terms. One common weight is:

<MathBlock latex="\operatorname{tfidf}(t,d)=(1+\log f_{t,d})\left[\log\frac{1+N}{1+\operatorname{df}(t)}+1\right]" />

With vectors `v_q` and `v_d`, cosine similarity is:

<MathBlock latex="\operatorname{cos}(q,d)=\frac{v_q^\top v_d}{\lVert v_q\rVert_2\lVert v_d\rVert_2}" />

TF-IDF is transparent and strong for exact terms, identifiers, and small corpora. It
can miss synonyms, spelling variation, and cross-modal meaning. The project includes
explicit tokenization and an all-zero query contract; it never returns zero-score
documents as if they were evidence.

### Dense retrieval

A dense retriever maps a query and document to vectors from a trained encoder:

<MathBlock latex="e_q=f_\theta(q),\qquad e_d=g_\phi(d),\qquad s(q,d)=e_q^\top e_d\;\text{or}\;\operatorname{cos}(e_q,e_d)" />

Approximate nearest-neighbor indexes make a large corpus searchable, but introduce
indexing, freshness, filtering, and recall choices. A dense vector can match meaning
without literal overlap and can also produce a confident semantic miss. This project's
`dense` baseline is not trained: a small `ALIASES` table makes the semantic-ish behavior
inspectable and tests can reproduce it exactly.

### Hybrid retrieval and fusion

Lexical and dense scores have incompatible scales. An unsafe hybrid blindly adds raw
scores. Alternatives normalize each score distribution, learn a combination, or fuse
rankings. Reciprocal-rank fusion is simple:

<MathBlock latex="\operatorname{RRF}(d)=\sum_{r\in\text{rankers}}\frac{1}{k_0+\operatorname{rank}_r(d)}" />

The project demonstrates both a transparent score-average hybrid and RRF. Report which
one you used; “hybrid” is not a sufficient experiment description.

### Reranking

A first-stage retriever may return 20–100 candidates. A reranker reads each query/
document pair with a more expensive interaction and returns a better order. This is a
precision/latency trade-off:

```text
large corpus -> cheap top-N retrieval -> expensive candidate reranking -> top-k context
```

The lab's reranker uses canonical token coverage and bigram overlap. It is a rule-based
cross-encoder analogue that never invents a candidate. It is not a trained reranker.
Candidate preservation is a useful test because a reranker that introduces an unindexed
document is not merely “more accurate”; it has violated the pipeline contract.

### HyDE

Hypothetical Document Embeddings (HyDE) expands a query by generating a hypothetical
answer/document and embedding that text for retrieval. The intuition is that a document
style may match the corpus better than a short question. The risk is equally important:
the hypothetical text can hallucinate entities or details and pull retrieval toward
those inventions. The project uses a deterministic `hyde_query` seam that concatenates a
supplied hypothetical string. It does not call a language model and does not claim that
the supplied expansion is true.

### GraphRAG and knowledge graphs

A knowledge graph represents entities and typed relations. A simple edge set is
`(subject, relation, object)`. GraphRAG can retrieve seed documents, expand neighbors,
then combine graph context with text retrieval. It can improve multi-hop recall, but
entity resolution, edge provenance, relation direction, fan-out, freshness, and graph
construction errors become first-class concerns.

The fixture stores links such as `clip → vit` and `vae → diffusion`. `graph_expand`
walks a bounded number of hops, applies a propagation penalty, and tracks visited nodes.
This establishes bounded traversal on known links. It is not an enterprise graph, a
knowledge extraction system, or evidence that any named company uses a particular
GraphRAG architecture. A serious graph evaluation reports edge provenance and compares
no-expansion, one-hop, and multi-hop slices.

### ColBERT-style late interaction

A single pooled vector can blur which query term matched which document term. Late
interaction preserves token-level vectors. For query tokens `q_i` and document tokens
`d_j`, a toy max-sim score is:

<MathBlock latex="S(q,d)=\frac{1}{|q|}\sum_i\max_j\;\operatorname{sim}(e(q_i),e(d_j))" />

The reference implementation computes this with the fixed feature vectors. It is
ColBERT-like in the max-sim idea, not a ColBERT checkpoint, tokenizer, index, or
benchmark. The useful lesson is to inspect the interaction order: each query token
chooses its best document token before aggregation.

## 7. Evaluate retrieval before celebrating an answer

Let `R_q` be the set of positive documents for query `q` and `rank_q(d)` the position of
document `d` when it is retrieved. With a cutoff `k`:

<MathBlock latex="\operatorname{Recall}@k=\frac{|R_q\cap\operatorname{top}_k(q)|}{|R_q|}" />

Reciprocal rank uses the first relevant hit:

<MathBlock latex="\operatorname{RR}@k=\begin{cases}1/r,&r=\min\{i:d_i\in R_q\},\ r\le k\\0,&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}" />

Mean reciprocal rank is the average RR over queries. Average precision averages precision
at every relevant hit and divides by the number of relevant documents. The project uses
that denominator even when some relevant documents are not retrieved:

<MathBlock latex="\operatorname{AP}@k=\frac{1}{|R_q|}\sum_{i=1}^{k}P@i\,\mathbf{1}[d_i\in R_q]" />

NDCG supports graded relevance. With gain `2^{rel}-1`:

<MathBlock latex="\operatorname{DCG}@k=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{2^{rel_i}-1}{\log_2(i+1)},\qquad \operatorname{NDCG}@k=\frac{\operatorname{DCG}@k}{\operatorname{IDCG}@k}" />

The evaluator macro-averages over the union of query ids, so an absent ranking counts as
zero rather than disappearing from the report. Edge cases are part of the metric API:
empty relevance is zero, duplicate ranked ids are rejected, relevance must be finite and
nonnegative, and `k` must be positive.

Metrics still depend on labels. A qrel made by one annotator, an incomplete graph, or a
query set that excludes hard cases can make a system look better without making answers
more trustworthy. Add answer-level citation checks, abstention rates, latency, and
failure slices for a real application. Do not optimize only the average.

<KeyTakeaways>
- CLIP aligns image and text representations through a contrastive objective; ViT represents an image as positional patch tokens. Neither alignment nor patch attention guarantees truth or spatial completeness.
- VAEs optimize a reconstruction-plus-KL ELBO, GANs play a generator/discriminator game, and diffusion learns a reverse denoising process. The tiny NumPy fixtures demonstrate shapes and finite steps, not image quality.
- World/video models add temporal state, motion, and consistency requirements; a linked `video` document is not a video model.
- A voice-native system needs explicit ASR, query, retrieval, response, TTS, interruption, privacy, and telemetry contracts. Offline adapters can test the seam without pretending to recognize speech.
- TF-IDF, dense, hybrid, RRF, reranking, HyDE, GraphRAG, and late interaction answer different retrieval problems. Name the variant, candidate budget, index, and evidence boundary.
- Recall@k, MRR, MAP, and NDCG measure labelled ranking behavior. Add citation, abstention, latency, and failure-slice evaluations before claiming grounded answers.
- Public papers support named methods and releases, not undocumented company internals. Keep the seven CompanyLens entries release-specific and distinguish evidence from practice analogy.
</KeyTakeaways>
